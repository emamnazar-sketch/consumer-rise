A note from the founder

My name is Amam. I'm a father, a husband, and a son.

I'll be honest with you — I started Consumer Rising because I was confused. I'd stand in the grocery store staring at a label with no idea what half of it meant. Then the same thing at the pharmacy. They hand you a bag, you nod, you go home. Nobody explains anything.

So I started looking things up. Labels, studies, the actual documents from the regulators. And the more I read, the more I realized it wasn't just me. Nobody can figure this out alone. It's written in a way that keeps regular people out — whether that's on purpose or not, I'll let you decide.

I'm not a scientist. I'm not a doctor. I'm just a guy who got tired of not knowing what was going into his family's bodies, so he started reading.

That's all Consumer Rising is. Regularly, I send you what I found — about food, about medicine, about all of it. In plain words. With the sources attached so you can check me. And when the science isn't clear, I'll tell you that too.

A

What we stand for

  • Plain words. If a finding can't be explained simply, we haven't understood it well enough yet.
  • Shown sources. Every claim links to the regulator filing, the study, or the public record behind it.
  • Honest uncertainty. When experts disagree, we show the disagreement instead of picking a side for clicks.
  • Families first. We write for the parent in the grocery aisle at 6pm, not for the industry.
B

The problem we solve

Ingredient labels are written by lawyers, for lawyers. "Natural flavors." "Spices." Chemical names with numbers. The average parent has about forty minutes to shop for a week of food — nobody has time to decode a chemistry degree in aisle seven.

Meanwhile, ingredients banned or warning-labeled in Europe sit on American shelves with no warning at all. That gap — between what regulators elsewhere know and what American shoppers are told — is where Consumer Rising works.

We do the reading so you don't have to. One ingredient, one finding, at a time.
