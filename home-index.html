The home index · Phase 1

Cleaning products don’t have to list everything. Here’s what the studies say is in them.

We don’t rate your cleaning products. We reveal them. What stays in your home is up to you.

Food has a nutrition label. Cosmetics list every ingredient. Cleaning products — the sprays, soaps, and detergents used in every room of the house — were long exempt from telling you what’s inside. California’s Cleaning Product Right to Know Act (SB 258, 2017) began changing that, which is the only reason some of what follows is knowable at all. Below: 17 chemicals found in household products — what each one is, where it shows up, and what U.S. health agencies report about it. Every claim links to its source. No ratings, no verdicts, no “avoid” lists. The facts are the facts; you decide what they mean.

Methodology & disclaimer

How this index was built — and what it is not.

Methodology. Phase 1 covers 17 chemicals commonly found in household cleaning and laundry products, solvents, and air fresheners. Candidate chemicals were verified one by one against primary sources; candidates that could not be sourced to an opened primary document were dropped (see list below) rather than written from memory. Every fact carries a per-fact source link, opened and confirmed during research in October 2026. Sources: the Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry (ATSDR/CDC) ToxFAQs series, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), FDA final rules via the Federal Register, CDC NHANES biomonitoring documentation, and peer-reviewed literature via NIH/PubMed. Regulatory-status lines report only what Phase 1 sources verified; “Not covered in Phase 1 sources” means the EU or California record was not checked against an opened primary source in this phase — it is not a claim that no regulation exists. This index carries no safety ratings by editorial rule: it states what each chemical is, where it is found, and what studies and regulators report. Dropped for lack of openable primary sources in Phase 1 (candidates for a later phase): fragrance (as a label term), the 26 EU fragrance allergens, formaldehyde-releasing preservatives (DMDM hydantoin, quaternium-15, bronopol, imidazolidinyl urea, diazolidinyl urea), sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS), sodium laureth sulfate (SLES), parabens, cocamide DEA, DEA/MEA/TEA, phosphates, alkylphenol ethoxylates (NPEs), hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide, naphtha/petroleum distillates, optical brighteners, EDTA. General information only — not medical advice. Last updated October 2026.
